Pairs

Never take insurance or even money. A dot marks the three cells that change with the soft-17 rule: 11 vs A, A,7 vs 2 and A,8 vs 6.

The goal

Finish closer to 21 than the dealer without going over. Each player plays only against the dealer, so you're never competing with the people next to you.

Card values

Cards 2 through 10 count as marked. Jacks, queens and kings count 10. An ace counts 1 or 11, whichever helps.

A hand where the ace counts as 11 is called soft. A,6 is soft 17, and it can't bust on the next card. Any other hand is hard.

How a round goes

  1. Put your bet in the circle before the cards come out.
  2. Everyone gets two cards. The dealer gets one face up and one face down, called the hole card.
  3. If the dealer shows an ace or a 10-value card, they check for blackjack first. If they have it, the hand ends right there.
  4. You play your hand: hit, stand, double or split.
  5. The dealer turns over the hole card and draws until reaching 17 or more. At tables marked "dealer must hit soft 17," the dealer also hits A,6.
  6. Higher total wins. A tie is a push and you keep your bet.

Your options

Hit
Take another card.
Stand
Keep your total and end your turn.
Double
Add a second bet equal to the first and take exactly one more card. Only on your first two cards.
Split
With two cards of the same value, add an equal bet and play them as two separate hands.
Insurance
A side bet offered when the dealer shows an ace. Always decline it.

Some tables also let you surrender, giving up half your bet instead of playing. This trainer leaves it out.

Blackjack means an ace and a 10-value card as your first two cards. The 6:5 payout adds about 1.4% to the house edge, so read the felt before you sit down and look for 3:2.

Hand signals

In shoe games, where the cards are dealt face up, you don't touch your cards. The dealer reads your hand signals so the cameras can see your decision.

Hit
Tap the felt behind your cards.
Stand
Wave your hand flat over your cards.
Double
Place the extra chips beside your bet, not on top, and hold up one finger.
Split
Place the second bet beside the first and hold up two fingers in a V.

Hand-held games with one or two decks work differently: you pick up your cards with one hand, scratch them toward you on the felt to hit, and slide them under your chips to stand.

At the table

  • Check the minimum-bet sign before you sit.
  • Buy in by laying cash on the felt between hands. The dealer can't take money from your hand.
  • Once the cards are out, leave the chips in your circle alone.
  • Tipping the dealer is customary. Many players place a small bet for the dealer beside their own.
